The Kettle Spirit茶釜

An old iron kettle that grew legs after a hundred years of use. Drag to turn it round and look at it from every side. Then try tapping it, once while it stands still and once while it walks.

DRAG TO TURN · PINCH OR SCROLL TO ZOOM

The original

In Japanese folklore, a tool used for a hundred years gains a spirit and becomes a tsukumogami. This kettle has a face on its lid, a long nose that curls up like a trunk, and white whiskers under it.

Source: Tsukumogami-e, National Diet Library (public domain). The 3D model is a free interpretation; the back and sides are imagined.
